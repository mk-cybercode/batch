The concept01 / 07

Learn the craft. Prove the demand. Then build the brand.

01

What

Small-batch gelato made with Italian ingredients and equipment — machinery, flavour bases and a consulting chef through one supplier. Sold in 500ml tubs, with smaller cups for markets and events.

02

How it starts

Production runs from a home kitchen on a single commercial machine — made in advance, frozen, and sold through four channels: direct online, Uber Eats, stockists and markets.

03

Where it goes

A gelato & coffee store in a high foot-traffic location — and in time, a small group of stores supplied by one central kitchen.

Why this model02 / 07

Built to be hard to lose on.

Validate before scaling. Phase 1 proves the product and the demand at minimal fixed cost — a store carries rent, staff and a larger machine, and is justified only once the product has proven it sells.

01

Near-zero fixed costs

Home production. No rent, no staff, no salary drawn — the operator keeps a full-time job throughout. The business can trade slowly, or pause, at almost no carrying cost.

02

Made ahead, frozen

Gelato is churned in batches and sold over the following weeks — about 9 hours of production a week in year one, rising to around 12 by the second summer. Not tied to daily demand, so it fits around a job and later makes each store simple to run.

03

A hard downside floor

53% of the capital is one imported Italian machine with an established resale market. If demand never lands, the equipment is resold to recover most of the investment. Exposure is capped, not open-ended.

The channels05 / 07

Four ways to sell. One tub.

Each channel reaches a different customer. Markets function primarily as a brand-building channel — revenue is driven by tub sales, stockists and events.

Margin

Online & social

Highest margin — no commission. The 500ml tub and 3-pack, delivered. Builds the audience and following the store will later depend on.

Volume

Uber Eats

Immediate reach into a large base. Listed prices absorb the platform commission, so the amount received remains R190. Cold chain handled with dry ice.

Visibility

Stockists

Wholesale at ≈ R125 — lower margin, but steady baseline volume and shelf presence. Branded freezers placed as it scales.

Brand

Markets & events

Markets are the shop window; events are the best use of capacity — a 150-serving booking returns roughly R5,000 in profit.

Direct and events carry the margin. Platform and stockists carry the volume. Markets build the brand.

Downside, handledEach risk with a corresponding plan
Recipe development runs long

The Italian supplier provides a consulting chef and machine training. Operating costs are low, making a slower start affordable.

Consistency between batches

Italian stabilisers control texture and shelf life. Recipes documented once finalised.

Product melts in transport

Dry ice and delivery costs built into pricing. Provision made for freezers at stockists.

Sales decline in winter

Launch into summer to establish a customer base. Stockists and events sustain winter trading.

Single ingredient supplier

Hold six to eight weeks of stock once recipes are set. Identify an alternative supplier.

Equipment failure

Purchased new with a one-year guarantee. Parts held locally. Insured.

Sales fall below target

Channels open sequentially. Repayments flex with actual profit — a slow month does not create debt.

Demand insufficient to continue

Low operating costs permit continued trading at minimal cost. If discontinued, equipment is sold.
